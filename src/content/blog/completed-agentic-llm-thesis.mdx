I submitted my master's thesis at the Technical University of Munich in September 2026, following six months of work at Infineon. The full title is **An Agentic LLM System for Executive Decision Support over Hierarchical KPI Outlier Data**.

[Read the thesis online or download the PDF →](/thesis)

## The problem

A hierarchy of business metrics can reveal many deviations without telling a manager which ones belong in a short weekly report. Several views may describe the same underlying movement. Follow-up questions add another problem: business terms need to map to the right measures, time periods, and data.

## The system

I built three connected parts:

- **Shared semantic context:** expert-reviewed business definitions, time rules, and a searchable name lookup.
- **Weekly reporting:** bounded agents choose reporting levels and organize findings. Tools calculate contributions, combine overlapping views, and check the report's accounting.
- **Conversational analysis:** a tool-first agent answers business-language questions and follows up on published summaries. Progressive disclosure exposes relevant tools; requests that those tools cannot complete move to guarded, read-only Text-to-SQL.

The implementation uses Python, LangGraph, LangChain, DuckDB, and Impala/SQL, with a Dash interface and scheduled report generation.

## What the evaluation showed

Agents changed some analytical choices and report groupings. Completed live-agent reports passed the applied numerical and reporting checks, but a shorter report did not always account for more of the movement.

The tool-first conversational configuration produced more fully correct answers and used fewer input tokens than direct Text-to-SQL on the evaluated questions. That was not a reduction in every kind of usage: output tokens and model calls increased. Incomplete answers, unsupported explanations, and clarification failures remained.

The distinction matters: tools can verify calculations, but that alone does not establish whether a report is useful to management. The thesis discusses these limits and the next steps for evaluation with users.
