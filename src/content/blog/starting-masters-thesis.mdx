I'm excited to share that I've started my Master's thesis at Infineon Technologies, focusing on **Large Language Models and AI Agents in Supply Chain**. This research sits at the intersection of two fields I'm deeply passionate about: AI and real-world systems engineering.

## Why Supply Chain?

Supply chain management is one of those domains where complexity compounds at every level. From demand forecasting to logistics optimization, the number of variables and interdependencies makes it a perfect candidate for AI-assisted decision making. What makes it particularly interesting is that most decisions aren't purely algorithmic — they require contextual reasoning, something LLMs are increasingly capable of.

## The Research Direction

The core question I'm exploring is: **can autonomous AI agents meaningfully improve supply chain operations?** Not just by generating reports or answering questions, but by actively participating in workflows — analyzing data, proposing decisions, and executing multi-step processes with appropriate human oversight.

I'm building on my experience with RAG systems (from the FlexfitAI project) and LLM API integration (from my work at Everstox) to create a practical framework that bridges academic research with industrial application.

## What's Next

Over the coming months, I'll be diving deep into agent architectures, tool-use patterns, and evaluation methodologies. I'm particularly interested in how we can build agents that are not just capable, but also reliable and transparent in their reasoning — critical requirements for any enterprise application.

Stay tuned for updates as the research progresses.
